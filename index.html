How-To

Reconstitution & instructions

How to mix your peptide with bacteriostatic water using a standard U-100 insulin syringe. Research use only.

The 1:10 rule

10 mg vial+100 units BAC water=1 mL @ 10 mg/mL

Adding 100 units (1 mL) of bacteriostatic water to a 10 mg vial makes a 1:10 solution. On a U-100 insulin syringe each “unit” = 0.1 mg (100 mcg) of peptide, so the marks on the syringe map straight to your measured amount.

VialBAC waterConcentrationPer 1 unit
5 mg100 units (1 mL)5 mg/mL0.05 mg
10 mg100 units (1 mL)10 mg/mL0.10 mg
15 mg150 units (1.5 mL)10 mg/mL0.10 mg
20 mg200 units (2 mL)10 mg/mL0.10 mg
  1. Wipe the stopper of the peptide vial and the bacteriostatic water with an alcohol prep pad.
  2. Draw 100 units (1 mL) of bacteriostatic water into a syringe.
  3. Insert the needle at an angle and let the water run slowly down the inside of the vial — don't blast the powder.
  4. Gently swirl (never shake) until fully dissolved and clear.
  5. Store reconstituted vials refrigerated; draw each dose with a fresh sterile syringe.
